# 100 polish and feel ideas (list E)

None of these repeat docs/ideas-200.md, ideas-200-b.md, ideas-200-c.md,
ideas-100-d.md, ideas-50-biomes.md or ideas-60-terrain.md. Each was searched
for in the code (Nov 2026) and isn't in the game yet. This list leans to
polish, comfort and getting ready for Steam, not new creatures or places.

## Settings and comfort (1–14)
1. Separate volume sliders: effects, animals, weather, ambience, menus (there's only one volume now)
2. Invert mouse up/down
3. Frame-rate limit (30 / 60 / 120 / unlimited), to save battery and keep laptops cool
4. Resolution slider: draw the world at 50–100% size and sharpen it, for slow computers
5. Automatic quality: grass and shadow distance drop when the frame rate falls, and come back when it's steady
6. Hold or toggle for sprint and crouch, your choice
7. Interface size slider (bigger HUD and menus for TVs and the Steam Deck)
8. Brightness check on first launch: "turn it up until this symbol is just visible" (nights are very dark)
9. Pause on its own when the window loses focus (single player)
10. Comfort options: turn off camera shake, the sway when hurt, and other camera motion
11. Fewer-flashes option for lightning, eruptions and explosions
12. Warning when two actions are set to the same key
13. Left-handed: tools held in the left hand
14. An easier-to-read font option

## Feedback and feel (15–32)
15. A red mark at the screen edge pointing to whatever just hit you
16. A soft thunk and a flick of the crosshair when an arrow or spear lands
17. A thin health bar over an animal for a few seconds after you hit it
18. Full item tooltips on hover: what it does, food and drink value, durability left, weight
19. A tool about to break: its icon flashes and it creaks, before it snaps
20. A tool breaking: it snaps in two with splinters and a crack
21. Dropped items nearby drift towards you when you walk over them
22. "+3 Wood" style notes stacking at the screen's side as you gather
23. The camera dips and dust puffs up when you land from a big fall
24. Your held tool swaying and bobbing in step with your walking speed
25. The crosshair changes over things you can use (a hand over items, an axe over trees)
26. Use prompts that show the key: "E  Pick up flint"
27. Warning sounds before hunger and thirst start hurting (a stomach growl, a dry swallow)
28. Sleeping shown as a time-lapse: the sun or the stars sweeping over, then waking
29. A large title fading in the first time you enter a biome ("Ancient Oak Wood")
30. A chime and a note the first time you find a landmark
31. A morning card when you wake: "Day 12 · Spring"
32. Weather heard before it arrives: the wind rising, a hiss of rain coming over the trees

## Sound and music (33–41)
33. A quiet soundtrack: a few notes at dawn and dusk and when you find a place, silence otherwise
34. A tense sting when a predator starts stalking you
35. Each region its own instruments (a flute in the mountains, drums in the jungle)
36. Music on the title screen
37. Echoes outdoors: shouts and blows echoing in canyons and valleys, sound deadened in deep snow
38. Clicks and page-turn sounds in the menus and the journal
39. Your character's voice: grunts when hit, a gasp on surfacing, chosen on the Character page
40. Sounds muffled when a hill is between you and them
41. Your own footsteps louder indoors on wooden floors

## Menus, saves and first steps (42–55)
42. Tips and animal pictures on the loading screen
43. A "What's new" panel on the title screen after each update
44. The world list showing a small picture of where you last stood, time played and day count
45. Save backups: the last three kept, and a broken save rolled back to the one before
46. "Save now" in the pause menu, with a "Saved" note
47. A small card with the five most-used keys the first time you play
48. Hints the first time something happens ("It's raining: get under a roof or tree to stay dry")
49. A statistics page: days survived, distance walked, animals hunted, deepest cave, highest peak
50. A credits screen for the whole game (now there's only the sound credits link)
51. Translations into other languages, starting with the biggest Steam ones
52. A "Report a problem" button that sends the seed, position, settings and a screenshot
53. Restore after a crash: offer to load the last autosave
54. A benchmark: a short fly-through that measures the frame rate and recommends settings
55. Graphics presets named for computers ("Laptop", "Gaming PC", "Steam Deck")

## The living world (56–70)
56. Herd animals lifting their heads and watching you a moment before they bolt
57. Birds bursting out of the trees when a tree falls or something big roars
58. Dust motes drifting in the light under forest trees
59. Snow settling on your figure's shoulders and hat in third person, shaken off indoors
60. Your figure's clothes darker and wet-looking after a swim or in rain
61. Your figure's hair and cloak blowing in strong wind
62. Trees dropping a few leaves when you brush past or chop them
63. Branches bending aside as you walk through bushes
64. Grass and leaves stuck to you after lying in the grass or crawling
65. Insects swarming round a light left burning at night
66. Animals startled by a gunshot-loud noise (a falling tree, a slammed door) running in every direction
67. Little birds hopping on the ground near you when you stand still a long while
68. Cobwebs forming in corners of buildings you haven't visited in a long time
69. Dust falling from a roof in an earthquake while you're indoors
70. Your fire's light flickering on nearby tree trunks, moving with the flames

## Playing together (71–82)
71. Text chat
72. A player list showing everyone's connection quality (Tab)
73. Join by a short code a friend reads out to you
74. An arrow at the screen edge pointing to each friend, and their names on the map
75. Point at a spot and press a key to mark it for friends for a few seconds
76. Map discoveries shared between friends in the same world
77. Host controls: remove a player, set the difficulty, set the time of day
78. Night skipped only when everyone is in bed
79. A trading window to swap items face to face
80. Titles earned and shown under your name ("Bear-slayer", "Mapmaker")
81. Friends' footprints and campfires shown faintly on your map once you've been near them
82. A shared world journal where everyone's discoveries are written

## Ready for Steam (83–100)
83. A demo: the first three days, or one region, for Steam's demo festivals
84. A trailer camera: smooth flights you set up through a world, for filming
85. Share a seed as a link with a postcard picture of its first view
86. A daily seed everyone plays the same day, with a scoreboard for days survived
87. Friends see what you're doing in Steam ("Exploring Ancient Oak Wood, day 12")
88. A world-presets file people can share (their own mixes of biomes and settings)
89. Steam trading cards and profile backgrounds drawn from the game's views
90. A soundtrack you can buy alongside the game, once there's music
91. The game opening straight into the last world played, with a "Continue" button
92. Remembering the window size and screen it was on
93. A proper icon, and a loading picture while the game starts
94. Showing which settings slow the game most, next to each one in Settings
95. A "what changed" note when Settings are changed mid-game that need a restart
96. A first-launch question: "Have you played survival games before?" setting hints and difficulty
97. Gentle quit: "Your world was saved 40 seconds ago" when you leave
98. Your own world's map shown behind the title menu, slowly panning
99. A small amount of data about crashes, sent only if the player agrees, to find bugs on computers like theirs
100. A "safe start" option that opens with the lowest settings, for players whose game won't start
