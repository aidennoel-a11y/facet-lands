# 60 terrain and exploration ideas

None of these repeat the earlier idea lists (ideas-200, -b, -c, ideas-100-d), and none are in the game yet.

## Rivers and water (1–16)
1. Braided rivers: wide gravel beds with many small channels splitting and rejoining
2. Oxbow lakes: crescent lakes left where a river cut off one of its bends
3. River islands with their own little woods, reached by wading or swimming
4. Gorges with a river roaring at the bottom and narrow ledges along the walls to edge along
5. Rapids: stretches of white water, rocks and standing waves, dangerous to boat through
6. Hanging valleys: side valleys that end high up a cliff, their streams falling off the edge
7. Cirque lakes: round, deep lakes in bowl-shaped hollows under the peaks
8. A staircase of small lakes stepping down a mountain valley, each spilling into the next
9. Plunge pools under waterfalls deep enough to jump into from the top safely
10. Lakes with an island holding something: a ruin, a nest, an old hut
11. Floating peat islands that drift slowly about a lake
12. Turquoise glacier lakes and rare red or pink algae lakes
13. A boiling lake in volcanic country, steaming, its edge dangerous
14. Springs where a river comes up out of a cave mouth in a hillside
15. Disappearing rivers that sink into the ground and come out again further on
16. Natural tunnels where a river runs right through a hill, walkable along its edge

## Coasts (17–19)
17. Estuaries where a river meets the sea, with mudflats and wading birds
18. Barrier islands: long sandy islands with a calm lagoon behind
19. Sandbars and spits curving out into the sea, walkable at low tide

## Mountains and rock (20–34)
20. Moraines: long ridges of boulders and gravel left by old glaciers
21. Boulder fields at the foot of cliffs that you hop across, rock to rock
22. Scree slopes that slide under your feet as you climb
23. Knife-edge ridges between peaks: a narrow, thrilling walk with drops both sides
24. A lost-world plateau: sheer-sided, with a different biome on top, reached by one hidden way up
25. Rock shelters and overhangs to camp under out of the rain
26. Avalanche chutes: bare strips down the mountainsides where the trees have been swept away
27. Snow cornices on the ridges that can break off under you
28. Wind-bent dwarf trees at the treeline, twisted flat by the weather
29. Glacier crevasses you have to jump or bridge
30. Ice falls: towering blue blocks of ice where a glacier tumbles down a steep step
31. Giant boulders with little caves underneath them
32. Rock pillars standing up out of rivers
33. Potholes: round holes swirled into riverbed rock, full of clear water
34. Rocks shaped like faces and animals, hidden about the world to find

## Volcanic and dry country (35–40)
35. Cinder cones you can climb up and walk down into the crater
36. Old lava flows now grown over with forest, pitted with holes where trees once burned
37. A maar: a ring of ash hills round a shallow crater with a meadow in it
38. White mineral terraces: stepped pools of warm, milky-blue water
39. Rock pools in desert canyons that hold rainwater: precious water to find
40. Dry washes: sandy, flood-cut gullies through the desert lined with tough trees

## Forest and land (41–50)
41. Glades deep in the forest: a clearing with one ancient tree and wildflowers
42. Mossy boulder woods: old forest floor heaped with moss-covered rocks
43. Fallen giant trees bridging ravines, to walk across
44. River banks undercut by the water, tangles of roots hanging out over it
45. Abandoned orchards gone wild, old fruit trees among the woods
46. Dry-stone walls running across the hills from long-forgotten fields
47. An ancient quarry cut into a hillside, half-cut blocks still lying there
48. Animal trails: paths worn through the woods that lead you to water
49. Goat paths up cliffs that you can follow to places you couldn't otherwise reach
50. A great lone rock rising out of the plains, visible for many kilometres

## Discovery (51–60)
51. Named places: each valley, peak, lake and river gets its own name, shown when you arrive
52. Summit log: reaching each peak's top is recorded, with its height
53. A landform journal that fills in as you find each kind of feature
54. A sea of cloud seen from the high peaks at dawn, the valleys hidden under it
55. Rivers you can follow on the map from spring to sea, each with its name
56. A healing spring in a hidden glade, with an old stone basin
57. A secret island lake reached only by swimming underwater through a tunnel
58. Tufa towers: strange limestone spires standing in a salty lake
59. Wildflower carpets that cover a hillside for just a week in spring
60. Old boundary stones carved with marks, leading along a forgotten border
