# 100 ideas: exploring, progress, biomes, fun, and after the Keep (list F)

None of these repeat the earlier lists (see IDEAS.md). Each was checked
against the code (Nov 2026): none is in the game yet.

## Exploring (1–25)
1. Surveyor's brass markers on hilltops: stand on three and the map fills in the triangle between them
2. Vista stones: carved frames at fine viewpoints; stand there at the right moment (sunset, a full moon, fog in the valley) to add the view to an album
3. Summit flags: plant a flag with your name on a peak you're first to climb (friends see it)
4. Honeyguide birds that chatter and lead you to a wild bees' nest, if you follow
5. Ravens circling and calling over a fresh carcass: free meat, or a predator still at it
6. Rising warm air over dark rock, sunny slopes and big fires that lifts your glider higher
7. A bell or a drum heard faintly through thick fog, leading to a hidden shrine
8. Old sighting stones: line them up with a certain star at night and they point to something hidden
9. Underwater caves with pockets of air to breathe in, and things left there
10. A ruined village up in the canopy of giant trees, linked by rope walkways
11. A whale skeleton on the deep sea floor with its own little world of crabs and eels round it
12. Seed vaults: sealed stone rooms holding seeds of crops from other climates
13. Expeditions: long journeys recorded by name in your journal ("Across the Red Desert, 14 km in 3 days")
14. The heart of each biome: one unique natural wonder per kind of land, to find and stand in
15. A map table at home: a big framed map of everywhere you've been, with pins you place
16. Valleys walled in by cliffs that you can only reach by gliding in from one high peak
17. Stand on a geyser as it goes off and get thrown high into the air (open the glider at the top)
18. Humming crystal caves: strike the crystals in the right order and a hidden door opens
19. Mirror lakes: dead still at dawn, they show a reflection of a ruin that isn't there
20. A giant's game board: huge carved stone pieces standing on a plain, mid-game
21. Colossal statues half-buried in jungle or sand, their faces worn away
22. Wind caves that blow a steady draught: follow it to find where else they open
23. Gold panning in mountain streams: glints in the gravel
24. Moon gates: stone rings where the full moon rising frames a far-off landmark
25. Rare wandering animals (a white stag, a golden bear) that are seen once in a long while

## Getting better (26–45)
26. Ambitions: three random goals for each new life ("tame a horse before winter"), each with a reward
27. Edges that dull with use; a whetstone puts the edge back
28. Handles wrapped in leather or sinew so tools last longer
29. A cooking skill: the better you are, the more good each meal does you
30. A building skill: stronger walls, and fewer materials for big builds
31. A taming skill: animals trust you sooner the more you tame
32. An herb-lore skill: you learn which plants heal by trying them
33. A weather skill: read the clouds and the wind to know tomorrow's weather
34. Mentor stones: carved tablets in old ruins, each teaching a skill a step once
35. Knowledge from discoveries: each new animal, place and plant found gives points to spend on small lasting know-how
36. A home's comfort (a fire, a bed, a rug, walls and a roof, light) giving you "well rested" for longer
37. A varied diet: eating many different foods in a few days gives you more stamina
38. Foals that take after their parents' speed and colour, so you can breed a faster line
39. Animals near your home growing used to you over time, and not fleeing as far
40. Waystations: small shelters you build along your routes that you can choose to wake at
41. Fishing through a hole in lake ice in winter
42. Your hunting record: bring down every kind of animal once for a lasting bonus against all of them
43. Old guild seals found in ruins that let you make one masterwork tool of each kind
44. Tool sets: a matching axe, pick and knife of the same metal give a small bonus together
45. Your character's hands and face weathering with the days survived (scars and calluses that also mean toughness)

## New lands (46–65)
46. Wisteria valleys: purple blossom hanging in curtains from old trees in spring
47. Larch forest: soft green in summer, blazing gold in autumn, bare in winter
48. Patterned tundra: frost-heaved polygons and stone circles across the flat ground
49. Warm-spring forest: trees round hot springs that stay green and snowless all winter
50. Polar dry valleys: bare gravel, frozen lakes, nothing growing, the bones of old seals
51. Star dunes: a sand sea of huge, many-armed dunes, the biggest in the world
52. Penitentes: fields of tall blades of snow and ice on the high plateaus
53. Monkey-puzzle forest: spiky, umbrella-shaped trees on volcano slopes
54. Tree-fern gullies: damp ravines under giant tree ferns
55. Quiver-tree desert: fat-trunked aloe trees on orange rock
56. Joshua-tree desert: shaggy, branching yucca trees over wide flats
57. Arctic willow scrub: knee-high tangled willow on the tundra edge, where ptarmigan hide
58. Snow-gum woodland: twisted white and red-barked gums above the snow
59. Seas of rock: mountain tops covered in a jumble of shattered stone blocks
60. Temperate rainforest: giant spruce and cedar dripping moss beside a cold sea
61. Kopjes: piles of huge granite boulders on the savanna, where lions lie in the shade
62. Granite domes: huge bald rock domes and sheer walls above meadow valleys
63. Wild sunflower plains: fields of sunflowers that turn to follow the sun
64. Red autumn tundra: the low bilberry and heath turning scarlet for a few weeks
65. Salt-pan islands: white salt islands in a shallow turquoise sea

## Just for fun (66–85)
66. Skipping stones on calm water, counting the bounces (your best kept)
67. Snowballs to throw at friends, and snowmen to build
68. Horse races round checkpoints you set up, with times kept
69. A hot-air balloon you build to drift slowly over the land
70. Toboggan runs on snowy slopes and slides down glacier ice
71. A tame fox that brings you shiny things it finds
72. Festivals through the year: a midsummer bonfire, a harvest feast, midwinter lights, each with a small treat
73. Fireworks made from saltpetre and sulphur, for celebrations
74. A board game carved in stone at your base, to play with friends
75. Raft runs down rapids, with times kept
76. Hide a chest and draw a map for a friend to find it
77. Cooking freely: put any ingredients in the pot and discover named dishes
78. Grilling over the fire with timing: take it off at the right moment for a perfect meal
79. Fireflies caught in a jar to use as a soft lamp
80. A biggest-fish record for each lake and river, shown on a board at home
81. Fishing up odd things: old boots, rusty keys that open a chest somewhere
82. Herding sheep into a pen with your dog
83. A photo mode: free camera, the world paused, soft focus and frames
84. A bird-watching list, filled in with a spyglass
85. Carved wooden toys and figures to set on shelves (just for looks)

## After the Lost Keep (86–100)
86. The Drowned Crown: the Keep's lit beacon points to a city under the great lake; open old sluice gates to drain it and go in
87. The Wardens' tombs: five knights' tombs across the world; laying each to rest gives a piece of their armour
88. The Starfall: soon after the Keep, a great star falls far off and leaves a new crater country of glowing crystal and strange life
89. The Hollow Mountain: a city carved inside the highest peak, opened with the Keep's key
90. The Sky Bridge: an old stone bridge over a vast chasm to a plateau no one could reach before
91. Siege nights: the crown draws the beasts of the land; defend the Keep through waves of them
92. Raising the young of a great beast, found in its den, until it's big enough to ride
93. The Weather Shrine: once a year, choose the next season (a long summer for crops, a short winter)
94. Your heir: your next life begins as your heir, at the Keep, with its banner and a family journal
95. The Keep's map table: endless expeditions made up for you (find this, in that land, within so many days), with rewards
96. The Frost Herald: a giant of ice that walks the tundra every winter after the Keep
97. Outposts: rebuild other ruins into outposts and link them with roads that are safer to travel
98. A harbour below the Keep: once restored, trading ships arrive now and then with goods from far lands
99. The arena of the old kings: fight remembered great beasts in the Keep's arena, round after harder round
100. Healing the land: blighted places (a dead forest, a poisoned river) recover one by one as you finish the Keep's tasks
