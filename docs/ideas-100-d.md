# 100 more ideas (list D)

None of these repeat docs/ideas-200.md, ideas-200-b.md or ideas-200-c.md, and none are in the game yet.

## Animals (1–20)
1. Musk oxen that form a horns-out ring round their calves when wolves come
2. Giraffes browsing savanna treetops, visible over the grass from far off
3. Zebra herds that bunch up so their stripes confuse a chasing lion
4. Rhinos with poor eyesight that charge at sounds rather than at you
5. Ostriches you can't outrun, which kick hard when cornered
6. Meerkat lookouts that bark an alarm when a hawk or lion is near (a free warning for you too)
7. Pikas whistling from rock piles above the treeline, stacking hay for winter
8. Marmots sunning on boulders that dive into burrows with a shrill whistle
9. Peacocks in the monsoon forest whose screams mean a tiger is close
10. Dhole packs: red wild dogs in the monsoon forest that hunt by whistling to each other
11. Red pandas asleep in bamboo-forest branches
12. Pangolins that roll into armoured balls when touched
13. Giant tortoises on remote islands, slow enough to ride on
14. Mudskippers hopping on mangrove mud at low tide
15. Fruit bats streaming out of the mangroves at dusk like a dark river in the sky
16. Army-ant columns crossing the jungle floor: step aside or get swarmed
17. Leafcutter ant trails of moving green leaves you can follow back to the nest
18. Snow leopards on the highest crags, seen only as a shadow on the rocks
19. Bighorn rams clashing heads in autumn, the crack echoing down the valley
20. Winter coats: hares, foxes and ptarmigan turn white in snow and brown again in spring

## Plants and food (21–35)
21. Banana plants in the jungle; the giant leaves also work as an umbrella
22. Strangler figs that fruit all at once and draw every animal for miles (a great hunting spot)
23. Cacao pods in the jungle, ground into a hot chocolate drink that warms you
24. Wild pepper vines in the monsoon forest: peppered meat spoils more slowly
25. Ginger root that settles your stomach after bad food
26. Bog moss as a clean wound dressing
27. Chestnut trees: roast the nuts in the fire in autumn
28. Bumper acorn years: some autumns the oaks drop huge crops and boar and deer get fat and plentiful
29. Grafting: join a cutting from a good apple tree onto a wild one for better fruit
30. Coppicing: cut trees regrow from the stump as straight poles after a few seasons
31. Willow twigs woven into baskets for carrying berries and eggs
32. Crop blight: a warm, wet spell rots potatoes unless you rotate your fields
33. Seed saving: keep the best of each harvest so next year's crop grows bigger
34. Butter churn: milk into butter that makes cooked food more filling
35. Earth oven: food wrapped in leaves and buried with hot stones, slow-cooked into a big feast

## Fire and survival (36–47)
36. Seasoned firewood: fresh-cut wood smokes and burns badly; stacked wood dries in a few days
37. Splitting maul and wedge to turn logs into firewood
38. Fire shapes: teepee for quick heat, log cabin for a long burn, star fire to save wood
39. Hidden fire pit that shows no flame at night, so nothing sees your camp
40. Banking the fire: bury the embers in ash at night and blow them back to life at dawn
41. Hot stones in your bedroll on freezing nights
42. Mosquito net over your bed in wetlands and jungle so you sleep through the night
43. Smoky fire of green leaves that drives off biting bugs
44. Woven straw rain cape that keeps you dry while you walk
45. Wading staff: deep, fast rivers push you downstream unless you brace with one
46. Hollow-reed snorkel to hide underwater from a predator
47. Glacier rivers that rise every afternoon with meltwater, so you cross at dawn

## Getting around (48–55)
48. Skis for fast runs down snowy slopes
49. Ice skates for frozen lakes
50. Sand sled for sliding down big dunes
51. Surfing waves on a wooden board
52. Swim fins sewn from hide
53. Pole vault over streams and gaps with a long pole
54. Belly-crawl through tight cave squeezes (slow, but the only way through)
55. Draw your own trails onto the map

## Building (56–67)
56. Wattle-and-daub walls: woven sticks plastered with mud, cheap and warm
57. Adobe bricks dried in the desert sun, keeping homes cool by day
58. Rammed-earth walls packed into a wooden frame
59. Turf roof that grows grass and flowers on top
60. Yurt: a round felt tent you pack up and carry on a horse
61. Tipi with smoke flaps you turn to suit the wind
62. Cliff dwellings: carve rooms into soft rock walls
63. Bamboo pipes carrying water downhill to a trough or fountain
64. Bamboo scaffolding for building tall without ladders
65. Living hedges that grow thick over a season and keep animals out
66. Wood types that matter: teak never rots (best for boats), redwood is light, oak is strongest
67. Pine tar to waterproof boats and roofs

## Weather, sky and seasons (68–84)
68. On misty peaks your shadow appears huge in the fog with a ring of light round its head
69. Moonbow: a pale white rainbow at waterfalls on full-moon nights
70. Sun dogs: two bright spots beside the sun on bitter days, warning of a cold snap
71. St Elmo's fire: blue glow on spear tips and masts in thunderstorms
72. Ball lightning drifting over the ground in a storm
73. Frost hollows: valley floors get much colder at night than the slopes, so camp up the hill
74. A warm, dry mountain wind that melts the snow in a single day
75. Icy night winds pouring down off glaciers
76. Steam rising off lakes on freezing mornings
77. Desert superbloom: after a rare rain the sand bursts into flowers for a few days
78. Spring pine pollen: a yellow haze in the air and a yellow film on ponds
79. Autumn leaf piles that crunch loudly (animals hear you) and that you can hide in
80. Mud season: thawed paths turn to mud and slow you down in spring
81. River ice breaking up in spring, jamming and flooding the banks
82. The first monsoon rain: a huge storm that wakes a deafening frog chorus
83. Harvest moon: a huge orange moon rising in autumn
84. Seed fluff drifting on the wind, showing which way it blows

## Places to find (85–94)
85. Living root bridges in the monsoon forest: fig roots grown across a river over centuries
86. Stepwells: deep stone wells with stairs down to the water in dry lands
87. Temples carved straight into cliff faces
88. Singing dunes that boom when you slide down them
89. A lone house-sized boulder on a flat meadow, far from any cliff, left by an old glacier
90. Pingos: round hills with a core of ice in the frozen north
91. Spider webs glittering with dew in meadows at dawn
92. Dunes that slowly drift, burying old ruins and uncovering others over the days
93. Ancient overgrown terraced fields on jungle mountain slopes that you can farm again
94. A smooth rock chute beside a waterfall you can slide down into the pool

96. Glider races through rings you set up in the sky
97. Hide-and-seek in an area you choose
98. A mailbox at your base where friends leave items and notes
99. Campfire stories: sitting round a fire together makes everyone rest faster
